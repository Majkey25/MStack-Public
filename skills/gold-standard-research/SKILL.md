# Gold standard research

## Find the evidence that can settle the question

Identify the claims that could change the answer. Specify the applicable version, date, population, or jurisdiction when it matters. Use primary or authoritative sources that can support each claim. For a software behavior, look for the current docs, source, or a reproduction. For an empirical claim, inspect the study, its method, and relevant replication or synthesis.

Read the passages you rely on. Check whether their scope matches the question. For a decision that depends on a contested result, look for the strongest counterevidence and compare assumptions or methods. Search snippets, titles, and abstracts are leads. Attribute only what the inspected material supports.

## Verify your own contribution

- Check units, calculations, assumptions, and limiting cases. For a proposed mechanism, derive a prediction and look for a counterexample or a small test.
- Keep source claims, deductions, and hypotheses separate. When sources disagree, explain the difference that matters to the answer.
- If an important claim depends on inaccessible material, seek a permitted equivalent or state the gap. Keep private material out of external queries unless authorized.

## Answer

Lead with the finding and the condition that matters most. Put citations next to the claims they support. Give enough detail to reproduce important calculations or follow a derivation. State uncertainty that would change the answer and name the evidence that could resolve it.
